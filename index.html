🦖 So, What Is This Place?

I'm Matthew — a cybersecurity professional who never stopped being completely fascinated by dinosaurs, fossils, evolution, deep time, and the strange experiments life has tried over the last few billion years.

Jurassic Matt is my public learning journal and personal prehistoric-life shrine. I'm using it to document what I'm studying, the courses and credentials I complete, fossil-hunting trips, specimens I find or acquire, museums and paleontological sites I visit, books I read, dinosaur research rabbit holes, and eventually the illustrated dinosaur book I want to create.

The goal isn't to cosplay as a professional paleontologist. The goal is to become a really good amateur one — and have an absurd amount of fun doing it.

Collection Philosophy

Document it.

Context makes a fossil more meaningful. Even a humble fragment deserves a record.

Identify honestly.

“Unknown mammal tooth” is better than confidently inventing a species-level ID.

Collect ethically.

Permission, provenance, applicable permits, and scientific value come before owning a cool object.

👤 About Jurassic Matt

Hi. I'm Matthew.

By day, I work in cybersecurity. Outside of that, I've been a dinosaur nerd for basically as long as I can remember. I love the science — anatomy, evolution, geology, taxonomy, fossilization, deep time — but I also just think dinosaurs are ridiculously cool animals, and I never want learning about them to become drab.

This site is my attempt to take that lifelong interest seriously without sanding all the fun off of it. I'm studying openly, documenting what I learn, getting involved in my local fossil community, building field experience, and slowly assembling a portfolio of the journey.

Maybe it stays a lifelong avocational pursuit. Maybe I contribute something useful someday. Maybe I publish a dinosaur book. Maybe I eventually find myself sweating in the Hell Creek Formation staring at a suspiciously bone-shaped brown object.

All of those outcomes sound pretty great.

Site Philosophy

Science without stuffiness.Accuracy matters. So does wonder.
Evidence over certainty theater.“We don't know” is a perfectly good answer.
