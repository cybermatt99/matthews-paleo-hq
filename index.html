Dinosaur Research Notes

I am building species-by-species notes that may eventually feed into a larger illustrated dinosaur book. My main rule is to separate direct fossil evidence from strong inference, reasonable reconstruction, and speculation.

Species Queue

001

Velociraptor mongoliensis

Feathers, quill knobs, skull anatomy, sickle claw, Djadochta environment, phylogeny, and popular depictions.

002

Tyrannosaurus rex

Growth, bite mechanics, Hell Creek ecology, integument, and historical reconstructions.

003

Spinosaurus aegyptiacus

Changing reconstructions, aquatic adaptations, tail anatomy, ecology, and ongoing debates.

004

Triceratops horridus

Cranial anatomy, horns and frill, growth, and Hell Creek ecology.

005

Deinonychus antirrhopus

Dromaeosaur anatomy, the dinosaur renaissance, and its influence on modern dinosaur culture.

About Me

I'm Matthew, a cybersecurity professional in Florida and a lifelong dinosaur fan. I have always been interested in paleontology, evolution, natural history, and the enormous timescales behind life on Earth.

In 2026 I decided to stop treating paleontology as something I only read about occasionally and start studying it more deliberately. That means coursework, books, local fossil collecting, museum visits, field trips, research notes, and eventually more ambitious experiences such as Hell Creek.

This site is not meant to present me as a professional paleontologist. It is a record of an amateur learning the subject seriously and enjoying the process.

Research standard: when I write about prehistoric animals, I try to distinguish what fossils directly show from what researchers infer and what remains uncertain.

Independent personal site. Not affiliated with any film studio, franchise, museum, university, fossil organization, or government agency.
